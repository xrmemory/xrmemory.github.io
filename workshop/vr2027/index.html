A shared question

What should count as XRMemory?

XRMemory investigates how spatially situated experiences can be captured, represented, preserved, and re-experienced through XR.

Our central task is to develop a provisional shared definition. Which relationships among experience, spatial context, embodiment, and interaction distinguish XRMemory? Cases from lifelogging, telepresence, digital twins, and memory augmentation will help us test the boundaries.

We welcome examples and counterexamples, evidence and uncertainty. The aim is a usable framework that records both agreements and unresolved differences.

Why IEEE VR?

Defining XRMemory requires examining embodied re-experiencing and 3D interaction alongside the systems that enable them. IEEE VR brings together the immersive-systems, interaction, and human-factors perspectives needed to connect these questions.

What we aim to build

A framework we can work with

  1. A provisional definitionCriteria, examples, counterexamples, and boundaries.
  2. A shared concept mapConnections among capture, representation, replay, and interaction.
  3. A research and writing agendaEvaluation priorities and an annotated outline for a joint position paper or white paper.

One continuing conversation

From discussion to a shared position

The series spans IEEE VR 2025, IEEE ISMAR 2025, and IEEE VR 2026. The forthcoming ISMAR edition and proposed fifth workshop form the next steps in this cumulative inquiry.

01

Explore at ISMAR 2026

The fourth workshop will prioritize participatory discussion, documenting emerging insights and open questions.

02

Develop at IEEE VR 2027

Returning and new participants will critically examine that material, test candidate definitions, and draft a shared framework and publication outline.

03

Write together afterward

A participant-reviewed synthesis is planned within four weeks. Volunteer writing leads will develop a fuller position paper or white paper with interested contributors.

The organizers’ continuing collaboration connects spatial capture and reconstruction, immersive interfaces, perception, and social interaction. This continuity helps translate technical cases into common concepts and evaluation questions.

A working session

Bring a case. Challenge a definition.

Short talks provide material for collaborative work. Organizers will circulate any documented ISMAR insights beforehand; mixed groups will test definitions on shared cases and build the outline of a joint paper or white paper.

Call for contributions · Preview

Help shape the research space

We plan to invite papers of 2–6 pages, excluding references, that can stimulate discussion and help define XRMemory.

Position / Vision

Conceptual arguments, definitions, provocations, or research agendas.

Research

Completed or preliminary evidence, methods, systems, and findings.

Survey / Synthesis

Literature, taxonomies, research gaps, and evaluation approaches.

Project / System

Ongoing projects, datasets, tools, prototypes, and infrastructures.

Bring something the group can work with: at least one example, counterexample, proposed criterion, or open question for the collective definition of XRMemory.

Topics of interest
  • Spatial, embodied, and multimodal experience capture
  • Spatiotemporal, semantic, scene-graph, neural, and world-model representations
  • Reconstruction, preservation, compression, retrieval, and persistence
  • Adaptive, personalized, and interactive re-experiencing
  • Memory, perception, cognition, emotion, and autobiographical experience
  • Avatars, agents, LLMs, and AI-mediated interaction
  • Visual, auditory, haptic, and embodied replay
  • Fidelity, authenticity, provenance, uncertainty, and trust
  • Evaluation, datasets, benchmarks, and reproducibility
  • Privacy, consent, ownership, identity, accessibility, and ethics
  • Training, telepresence, education, heritage, healthcare, collaboration, and personal memory

Planned submission and review

Manuscript
Original work in English, not under review elsewhere, using the IEEE Computer Society VGTC conference template.
Peer review
Single-blind: author names remain visible; reviewer identities are withheld. Review considers relevance, clarity, quality, and discussion value. Conflicted organizers will not handle affected submissions.
Participation
At least one author of each accepted paper is expected to attend in person. Non-author participants are equally welcome in discussion activities.
Submission system
Not open yet. The workshop will use an independently managed submission system. Its link will appear here once confirmed.
